# COURSE DESCRIPTIONS: the text shown on the play page, between the course tile and the course picker (one description per course).
#
# HOW THE FILE WORKS
#   - Each course starts with a line like  [woodbine] Woodbine Bend Golf Course
Bob Lohmann’s links-style layout (2002) rolls through the driftless farmland outside Stockton: a par 72 of about 6,200 yards from the gold tees (70.3/121) with mounds, tall native fescue, 31 bunkers, a creek and two ponds to keep us honest. Nineteen rounds in, Nick's nose becomes a faucet every year, Joe broke a club once, Kevin had diarrhea multiple times in the outhouse by #14, Craig always gets mad, and Andy aced #7 in 2026 and has told everyone. Our Galena!!! golfing home away from home.
#   - The description is the line(s) right under the [key] line, up to the next blank line. It can wrap over several lines; they are joined into one paragraph.
#   - Lines starting with # are notes and are ignored. That includes the "shown under it" and "trip champs" notes below each description.
#   - Straight quotes, curly quotes and apostrophes are all fine.
#   - An empty description hides the whole text block for that course.
#
# WHAT THE GAME ADDS UNDER EACH DESCRIPTION (not edited here)
#   - The rounds, years and trip champions come from the trip spreadsheet (the "trip" data in courses/courses.json). The # notes below each description show what
#     the game will print there, for reference only; editing those notes changes nothing.
#   - A course with "noTrip": true in courses.json (Augusta) shows only its description: no rounds line and no "Trip champs" line.
#   - Every course shows the same sections: the description, a "N rounds - years" line only when the crew has played it, and a "Trip champs" line always (the champions,
#     or "none yet, the Brown Jacket awaits" when the crew has not played it or no champion is recorded).
#
# APPLYING YOUR EDITS
#   - Push this file: the deploy copies it into the game automatically.
#   - Or run   python3 tools/course_text.py   to update courses/courses.json yourself (add --check to see what would change without changing it).
#   - The text wraps and does not shrink, so keep each description to about 500 characters (a longer one scrolls on a small screen).
#   - Do not run   python3 tools/course_text.py --export   unless you want to throw your edits away: it rewrites this file from courses.json.
#

[eagle_south] The South Course at Eagle Ridge
Roger Packard’s 1984 design at Eagle Ridge Resort plays through dense woods and a dramatic, hidden valley, with narrow fairways, big elevation changes and a par 72 of about 6,350 yards from the gold tees. Slope 136 and a 71.8 rating make it one of the hardest cards we play. This is where the trip began in 2007: four dudes, Kalle’s mom’s condo, and scores that peaked at 110.
# shown under it: 5 rounds - 2007, 2008, 2014, 2018, 2023
# trip champs: Brian '07 | Kalle '08 | Andy '14 | Nate '18 | Kevin '23

[eagle_north] The North Course at Eagle Ridge
Roger Packard’s 1977 woodland layout was the first course at Eagle Ridge Resort: a par 72 of about 6,400 yards from the gold tees (71.4/131) with big greens that break more than they look, and an 8th tee towering high above the green that needs a shot clipping the corner of Lake Galena. Three visits, three excuses: Brian’s 9:00 a.m. departure policy, Craig and Kevin flaming out early, and a skunk in a creepy house closet keeping everyone awake.
# shown under it: 3 rounds - 2009, 2017, 2019
# trip champs: Andy '09 '19 | Kalle '17

[legends] Legends at Memphis National Golf Club
An Arnold Palmer and Ed Seay design from 1971 at Memphis National, about 12 miles east of Memphis: a par 72 of about 6,300 yards from the white tees (70.5/126) with a creek running through it and houses lining the edges. One of Kalle's southern home courses. The crew hasn’t played it yet. Barbecue, Palmer pedigree and 1971 charm we’re old enough to appreciate; somebody add it to the rotation?

[eagle_general] The General at Eagle Ridge
Roger Packard and Andy North’s 1997 layout at Eagle Ridge, named for Galena’s Ulysses S. Grant, winds through old quarries and forest and is the toughest test on that property: slope 137 from the tips, and a par 72 of about 6,250 yards (70.6/132) from the 3-Star tees we play. We played it once, in 2011, on a huge discount after landslide inducing rains; the trip log reads “Hot tubbbbbbb" to commemorate the year. Priorities.

[fossil_trace] Fossil Trace Golf Club
Jim Engh’s 2003 design on a former clay mine at the foot of the Rockies, in Golden, with real dinosaur tracks in the sandstone on 12, big-contoured greens and a par 72 of about 6,250 yards from the blue tees (68.9/130). At nearly 5,800 feet, thin air adds about 8% to every carry in the game, our best shot at a long-drive trophy. Nate and Kalle played this with Andy while visiting him in Arvada, CO. It was glorious and followed up with various pickled things at local restaurants.

[stonehenge] Stonehenge Golf Club
A 1972 Charles Maddox design along IL 22 in Barrington, par 72 and nearly 6,850 yards from the tips (6,350 from the blue tees we play, 70.5/130). Close enough to Chicago for a Portillo’s or Lou Malnati’s Champions Dinner, which is honestly the whole pitch. This was Kalle's northern home course, and has been played by all the pals at various times. Nate got hit in the head by a shank on #2 once, sorry, Nate.

[thunderhawk] Thunderhawk Golf Club
A Robert Trent Jones Jr. design (1999), the Lake County Forest Preserves’ flagship on 243 acres of old farmland with oak and hickory groves and more than 7,000 yards from the tips (about 6,350 from the bronze tees we play, slope 134). Called “the benchmark” of Chicago public golf. Close to Wisconsin, so the cheese is always to blame. Andy, Kalle, Brian, Kevin, and Craig have all played it. Including one infamous round for Andy's birthday where it was 45 degrees and rainy with a strong wind.

[bearslide] Bear Slide Golf Club
Dean Refram’s design north of Indianapolis has a Scottish links-style front nine, a traditional back nine and a creek wandering through it, a par 71 of nearly 7,000 yards from the tips (about 5,950 from the white tees we play, 69.4/122), ranked the 14th best course in America by Golf Advisor in 2018. We played it on our one trip to Indiana (2024), and experienced a house bathroom with a car stereo and a day off for our knees.

[galena] Galena Golf Club
Just off US 20 outside Galena, a hilly, tree-lined layout that began as nine holes long ago and got its second nine in 1994: a par 72 of about 6,400 yards from the white tees (71.4/122), with plenty of long par 4s and passing trucks for spectators. Five rounds have been played here. Those years included a wiffle ball in the road, a keg of Coors Light, and 2021, when Woodbine had a tournament and we landed here.

[maxwelton] Maxwelton Braes Golf Resort
Door County’s historic 1929 layout by Roseman and O’Neil, tucked beside the Maxwelton Braes Lodge in Baileys Harbor, with Highway 57 cutting through the middle and dense woods on nearly every side. A par 72 of about 6,000 yards from the green tees (69.5/119), short enough to be friendly. It’s Andy’s home course, so he knows every bounce and isn’t telling.

[augusta] Augusta National Golf Club
MacKenzie and Jones built this in 1933: a par 72 of 7,555 yards from the Masters tees, with azaleas, pine straw, blindingly white bunkers and greens so fast and sloped that we would three-putt from the fringe and call it a good hole. Rae's Creek on 12 takes a sleeve of balls a round, Amen Corner takes the rest of the bag, and our best score here is a number we will carry to our graves. The pimento cheese is the only thing we would get right.
